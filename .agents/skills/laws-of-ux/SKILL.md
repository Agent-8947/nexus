# Laws of UX (NEXUS Edition)
**Source**: Jon Yablonski | **Version**: 2024 (2nd Ed)

## 🧠 Core Laws of Interaction

### 1. Jakob’s Law
Users spend most of their time on *other* sites. This means users prefer your site to work the same way as all the other sites they already know.
- **Rule**: Don't reinvent the wheel. Use familiar patterns for navigation, search, and interaction.

### 2. Fitts’s Law
The time to acquire a target is a function of the distance to and size of the target.
- **Rule**: Make interactive elements (buttons, links) large enough and place them close to where the user is likely to be.

### 3. Hick’s Law
The time it takes to make a decision increases with the number and complexity of choices.
- **Rule**: Minimize choices when response times are critical. Break complex processes into smaller steps.

### 4. Miller’s Law
The average person can only keep 7 (plus or minus 2) items in their working memory.
- **Rule**: Chunk information into groups of 5-9 items to avoid overwhelming the user.

### 5. Doherty Threshold
Productivity soars when a computer and its users interact at a pace (<400ms) that ensures that neither has to wait on the other.
- **Rule**: Provide feedback within 400ms. If a task takes longer, use progress indicators or skeletal screens.
